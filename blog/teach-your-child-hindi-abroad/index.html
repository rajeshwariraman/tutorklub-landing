How to Teach Your Child Hindi When You Live Abroad

Seven habits that help children abroad keep their Hindi.

12 November 2026 · 5 min read

Many Hindi-speaking families in the USA, UK and elsewhere see the same pattern: the children understand Hindi at home, but they reply in English, and reading Devanagari feels out of reach. A few steady habits change that. Here is what works.

1. Set a daily “Hindi time”

Choose one fixed slot, such as breakfast or the drive to school, where the family speaks only Hindi. Fifteen minutes every day does more than an hour once a week.

2. Let grandparents be the reason

Video calls with grandparents give children a real reason to speak. Before each call, help your child prepare one or two sentences in Hindi to share.

3. Start Devanagari early and playfully

Begin with the vowels (swar) and a few consonants (vyanjan) at a time. Trace letters, spot them on food packets and festival cards, and keep it a game rather than homework.

4. Use stories, songs and cartoons

Panchatantra stories, bhajans and simple Hindi cartoons build vocabulary without feeling like study. Repetition of favourites is how words stick.

5. Tie Hindi to festivals and food

Diwali, Holi, Raksha Bandhan and family events are natural moments to use Hindi words for food, clothes and rituals. A language linked to happy moments stays alive.

6. Correct gently

Children who feel judged switch to English. Praise the attempt, repeat the sentence back correctly, and move on.

7. Add a teacher when home practice is not enough

Grammar, reading and writing need structure that busy parents cannot always give. A live class adds a teacher who corrects pronunciation in the moment and classmates to speak with.
