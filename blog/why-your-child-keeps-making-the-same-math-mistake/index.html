Why Your Child Keeps Making the Same Math Mistake

Repeated mistakes are rarely careless. Here is how to find the thinking behind them.

14 November 2026 · 5 min read

You have corrected the same mistake five times, and it still comes back. It is tempting to call it carelessness. In most cases it is not. A mistake that repeats is usually a wrong rule, applied consistently. Fix the rule and the mistake disappears.

A wrong answer is a clue

Take a child who writes 6 × 0 = 6. Marking it wrong teaches very little. Asking “how did you get 6?” often reveals the real story: the child learned times tables as a chant and, when the pattern broke, guessed. A simple picture fixes it: “6 bags with 0 apples in each. How many apples?”

Three common hidden rules

  • Fractions treated like whole numbers. 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5 comes from adding tops and bottoms. The child has not yet seen what a denominator means.
  • “Always take the smaller from the bigger.” In 52 − 17, the child does 7 − 2 = 5 and gets 45. They have not understood regrouping.
  • Keyword guessing in word problems. The child sees “more” and adds, even when the question asks how many more are needed.

What to do at home

  1. Ask before you correct. “Walk me through it” tells you more than any answer key.
  2. Look for the pattern. If the same error appears in three questions, it is a rule, not a slip.
  3. Go back to objects and pictures. Pizzas for fractions, coins for place value, bags of apples for multiplication.
  4. Check again a week later. A gap is fixed when the child gets it right without a reminder.

When to get help

If the same error keeps returning across topics, a diagnostic assessment helps. It maps each topic and shows which misunderstanding sits underneath the mistakes, so practice targets the real gap.
